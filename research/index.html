About

I’m a PhD student in Experimental Psychology at UC San Diego, where I work in the Language Production Lab with Victor Ferreira.

My research is about language production. I look at how speakers tailor what they say to their listener (audience design), and at the split-second timing of who speaks when (turn-taking).

Video calls make a useful natural experiment. Audio delay and a flattened visual channel disrupt the cues people normally use to coordinate, which shows what conversation depends on. I also study how people judge a speaker’s confidence from the way they talk, and, more recently, how large language models compare to humans on those judgments.

Before UC San Diego, I was a research technician at Washington University in St. Louis, where I also wrote an honors thesis on second-language vocabulary learning.

Research themes

  • 01

    Conversation over video calls

    How audio delay and a reduced visual channel affect turn-taking and how speakers come across. I run in-lab, two-person conversation experiments and measure the timing between turns (floor-transfer offsets).

  • 02

    Judging speaker confidence

    How listeners perceive a speaker’s confidence from how they talk, and how that changes when they read a transcript instead of hearing the audio.

  • 03

    Humans and LLMs as language judges

    Comparing large language models to human raters on confidence judgments of dialogue. Some models track human judgments closely; others get pulled off course by whether the answer being given is actually correct.
